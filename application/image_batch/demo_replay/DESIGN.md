# Offline measured replay

The demonstration explains how a compute-side digital twin observes a cellular-connected service, forecasts arrivals, evaluates capacity options and steers worker admission. It replays completed physical evidence without running experiments, fitting forecasts, simulating alternatives or applying control. The capture uses KPN 5G trace emulation. Its 6G relevance is service orchestration and future integration with partner network twins and DevOps platforms; it does not measure 6G radio behavior. The [parent scientific design](../DESIGN.md) explains the workload, observation model, forecasting, simulation and physical controller. This account focuses on what an evidence replay can establish.

## Evidence and causal time

Every playback view shares one authoritative cursor. Physical evidence becomes visible only after its availability is established; published forecasts can extend beyond that cursor. Earlier observations initialize backlog and causal history before the visible operating start. Controller proposal timestamps conservatively bound publication of forecasts and alternatives because exact issue times are unavailable. Publication uncertainty is handled conservatively; observations without an established availability time remain unavailable.

Issued forecasts retain their original values, publication boundaries and horizons. A selected forecast can remain visible while later actuals mature, but later forecasts cannot replace its prediction. Seeking reconstructs the published prefix in either direction and excludes future publications. Retaining a forecast through a skipped update does not establish a new simulated decision. Recorded scores preserve the historical controller's assumptions rather than being recomputed by current software.

Short collection gaps can retain a coherent, already-published worker update with its age, within a bounded allowance and until explicit observer failure. This supports presentation continuity without filling missing samples. Scientific counters, histories, forecast quality and decisions retain strict coverage. Completed-study comparisons are independent of playback time: they describe separate completed runs, not a policy change performed at the cursor.

## Resources and service

Application CPU and RAM requests describe capacity held for Jobs, separately from measured use. Requests persist through startup, processing and finishing until physical release. The application-capacity counter sums the recorded admission budgets of accepting workers; it represents neither free CPU nor measured utilization. Configured VM capacity outside that budget does not establish host-system use. Powered reserves remain powered, so admission changes do not establish shutdown or energy savings.

Available CPU samples are short-window rates; RAM samples are working-set memory. They describe application observations rather than whole-node utilization. Partial sums remain distinct from complete worker totals, and missing values remain missing. Different per-Job sampling windows can make aggregated CPU rates exceed nominal capacity. Request groupings do not imply CPU affinity or physical memory placement.

Service response runs from original API Job creation to terminal Kubernetes Job completion for the report's evaluated arrival cohort. Classifier finish remains a separate diagnostic because cleanup and terminal reporting can add time. The replay waits for published terminal evidence before confirming an outcome. Failures count as missed; unconfirmed outcomes remain pending. Its provisional percentage divides on-time confirmations by confirmed outcomes and excludes pending Jobs. The completed-study target uses the full evaluation cohort, including failures and unfinished work. A progress total can also include completions outside that service cohort.

## Predictions and decisions

Forecast comparisons align recorded arrivals and native expected counts to the selected forecast's intervals. Error uses elapsed intervals with continuous full collection coverage; partial groups and intervals crossing the visible operating start are excluded. An interval ending does not prove a complete arrival census, since later first observations can update recent recorded counts. Non-atomic Job/Pod membership gaps affect queue and placement completeness without necessarily establishing lost arrivals.

Expected counts and sampled futures answer different questions. The scenario minimum-to-maximum range describes the captured ensemble; it is not a calibrated confidence interval and need not contain the expected forecast. Selecting a scenario connects its arrival pattern to its worker and service predictions without changing the controller's recorded choice.

Each capacity action retains every captured scenario's deadline score against the service target; the recorded selection considers the lowest scenario score. CPU allocation is capacity committed over the recorded allocation window, in core-minutes, and remains separate from instantaneous predicted requests and measured use. Unavailable alternatives preserve their reason, distinguishing worker limits from missing evidence. A recorded choice can be vetoed, delayed or superseded. Unapplied alternatives remain hypothetical, and later physical outcomes describe the entire closed loop rather than independently validating those alternatives. Historical confirmation remains valid through later observation gaps while current freshness is reported separately.

## Policy comparison and limits

Static control provides predictable fixed capacity. Reactive control adapts to observed demand using configured rules. The forecast digital twin adds anticipation and explicit simulation of service and allocation choices, with model dependence and computational cost. Monitoring can support every policy. The measured reactive arm is a custom worker-admission rule, not Kubernetes HPA or a universal industry baseline. Comparing forecast plus simulation with reactive control evaluates the combined policy; isolating simulation's contribution requires a matched forecast-only ablation.

Measured comparisons require matched planned workloads, common accounting and evaluation windows, accepted captures, and explicit initial-capacity differences. Failed and unfinished Jobs remain in service denominators; completed-only latency is labelled accordingly. Allocation coverage bounds describe missing observations, not statistical confidence, energy or billing. Different workload seeds remain separate repetitions. The accepted demonstration is a selected development illustration rather than held-out validation. Numerical findings and exact configurations belong with the [experiment evidence](../OPENDT_HANDOFF.md#final-data-location-and-replay-handoff).
